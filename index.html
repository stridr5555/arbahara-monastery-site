Monastery of Abuna Hara Dengeel

The Monastery of Abuna Hara Dengeel has acquired a 10-acre property in Crandall, Texas. Your gift will help develop our permanent spiritual and community campus, beginning with Phase I improvements and continuing with future construction as funding becomes available.

Vision & Sanctuary

Our 10-acre property in Crandall will become a permanent, worship-centered campus for prayer, liturgy, hospitality, spiritual formation, and community life. With the land secured, we are preparing the site for responsible, phased development.

Sanctuary

At the heart of the Monastery of Abuna Hara Dengeel is a sacred vision: a permanent Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo sanctuary dedicated to worship, prayer, the Divine Liturgy, and the preservation of our ancient faith and traditions. Until the permanent sanctuary is completed, development will proceed in phases so that regular worship and spiritual life can grow on the property.

Spaces & Stillness

The monastery is envisioned as a peaceful place of prayer, reflection, and spiritual renewal. The 10-acre property will provide spaces where clergy, monastics, families, elders, youth, pilgrims, and visitors can step away from the pressures of daily life and experience worship, contemplation, fellowship, and the quiet beauty of a monastery environment.

Community Pulse

Fundraising supports Phase I property improvements, professional planning, essential infrastructure, and future construction as resources become available.

Planning Timeline

These are the major stages guiding the property from acquisition through phased development of the permanent monastery campus.

  1. Property acquired • 10 acres in Crandall

    The monastery has secured the land that will become its permanent spiritual and community home.

  2. Phase I • Site improvements & preparation

    Initial work will focus on planning, permitting, access, utilities, and the improvements needed to prepare the property for long-term use.

  3. Future phases • Construction & campus growth

    Construction will continue in carefully planned stages as funding becomes available, creating spaces for worship, gathering, hospitality, and monastery life.
